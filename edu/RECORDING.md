# Narration to record (33 lines)

1. Crack an egg into a pan and stir.
2. Nothing is lost: every bit of yolk is still in there.
3. But you will never stir it back.
4. The information about where the yolk was hasn't vanished. It has spread out.
5. Qubits do something similar. We simulated twelve on Moth's Atlas platform, with an emulator, not a real quantum chip.
6. We give the middle one a tiny nudge.
7. To see where the nudge goes, we run an echo test.
8. Run the qubits forward a few steps, nudge the middle one, then run the same steps backwards.
9. With no nudge, every qubit would come home exactly. So we score each qubit with a number, F.
10. One means the nudge never reached it. Near zero: its trace is smeared across many qubits. Negative: flipped.
11. Physicists call this an out-of-time-order correlator, or OTOC.
12. Now watch the nudge spread.
13. Each step, it can reach at most one more neighbour on each side.
14. That makes a cone shape, called a light cone. After six steps it reaches both ends of the row.
15. First, a control.
16. We turn one dial so the two-qubit gate becomes a Clifford gate: a special, predictable kind.
17. Now the nudge never spreads at all.
18. Every other qubit comes back perfectly; the middle one just flips. Nothing is scrambled.
19. Now the scrambling setting.
20. Inside the cone, the echoes fade toward zero, and many turn negative.
21. The information isn't destroyed; every step can still be run backwards.
22. But it's hidden in patterns shared by many qubits, so no single qubit can show it.
23. That is scrambling, nicknamed the quantum butterfly effect. Only a metaphor.
24. Around step thirteen to sixteen, part of the echo comes back.
25. This simple chain is exactly solvable: signals bounce off the ends and partly return.
26. Add one more field, also computed on Atlas, and the chain is no longer solvable. The echo fades and settles, with no revival.
27. Why care?
28. Black holes are thought to be nature's fastest scramblers, and echo tests help physicists study that idea.
29. The same measurement helps tell chaotic quantum systems from solvable ones, as you just saw.
30. And it helps check that a quantum computer is doing genuinely complex quantum work.
31. These maps are real results from Moth Quantum's Atlas platform: the otoc-echo engine, on a noiseless emulator.
32. Same recipe, two settings: in one the nudge stays put, in the other it scrambles.
33. That's why you can't unscramble an egg.
