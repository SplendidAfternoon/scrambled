Explorer Game

You can't unscramble an egg.
Can a qubit chain?

Nudge one qubit in a chain, run the chain forward, then run it backwards. If nothing spread, the echo comes back clean. If the dynamics scramble, the nudge smears across every qubit and the echo at each site fades. That fading is the out-of-time-order correlator F(site, t), the "quantum butterfly effect". Every number on this site was measured with Moth Quantum's otoc-echo-v1 engine on the Atlas platform (emulator backend).

SCRAMBLED Explorer

Set the drive angles, measure a chain live on Atlas with your own key (or browse the measured runs), and watch the eggs scramble strip by strip as you scrub through time.

Don't Scramble the Egg

A short arcade game. Pick the pan, watch the quantum light cone race toward your eggs, and serve at the moment the echo comes back whole. Five levels, each backed by a chain measured on Atlas.

What is measured

A kicked Ising chain (RX by θx, RZZ by θzz per layer) of qubits, 12 in the game's runs. The centre qubit gets a Z kick between the forward and backward halves. F(site, t) is the echo measured at each site after t layers. It starts at ±1, stays there outside the light cone, and decays once the perturbation has spread. In a finite chain it partly revives (around t ≈ 13–16 for 12 sites) when the spreading front reflects off the ends.

The control

At θzz = π every gate is a Clifford. Cliffords map Pauli operators to single Pauli strings, so the nudge never spreads into a superposition of strings: F stays at ±1 forever. Only the kicked qubit flips sign. No scrambling, by construction. The explorer's θzz slider snaps to π so you can see it.

Honesty notes

All runs use Atlas's aer emulator with exact expectation values. No quantum hardware was used. The blur and morph images come from Atlas's blur-v1 and telablur-v1 engines. Cutting them into strips and crossfading in the browser is classical compositing. Job ids for every run are listed in the app and in the project's web/README.md.
